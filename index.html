Redbelly KYC and wallet activation, in plain language

One page for the questions the community asks again and again. Find your situation, get a clear answer, and follow the steps to an activated wallet.

Two different regional limits exist. Keep them apart.
The DAO
The DAO no longer restricts which regions can join. Anyone can join the DAO from any region.
Redbelly Network
The network still does not offer services in certain jurisdictions, listed in clause 15.1 of its terms. This is unchanged. See Question 4.

How to read the labels: OFFICIAL comes from an official Redbelly or Averer page. DAO BRIEF comes from the DAO’s task brief. COMMUNITY REPORTED and unconfirmed mean no official page confirms it yet. Every source is in the source list.

Part A

Do I need KYC?

KYC here means Redbelly Access, with verification by Averer. It gives your wallet permission to send transactions on Redbelly Network. Find your row.

Your situationAnswerWhy
I hold RBNT on an exchangeNOT NEEDEDRedbelly KYC is for network access. The exchange has its own verification rules, which are separate. [1]
I hold wrapped RBNT on another chain (Ethereum, Base, Polygon, BSC and others)NOT NEEDEDThose are other chains, not Redbelly mainnet. The access credential gives write access to Redbelly Network only. [1] [7]
I bridge onto Redbelly mainnet (receiving)UNCONFIRMEDNo official page says whether an unverified wallet can receive bridged RBNT. To be safe, verify and enable your wallet before you bridge. Anything you do after arrival needs it anyway.
I bridge off Redbelly mainnet (sending back to another chain)NEEDEDThe transaction is sent from your Redbelly wallet, so the wallet must be enabled. [1] [2]
I use Redbelly mainnet (send, swap, use apps)NEEDEDUsers must claim an access credential before they can self-enable write access. [1] [2]
I stake on Redbelly mainnetNEEDEDStaking is a transaction on the network. Official staking is a contract call on Redbelly. [8] Staking on an exchange follows that exchange’s rules.

Native vs wrapped: the one-line test

Native RBNT lives on Redbelly mainnet. Wrapped RBNT is a copy on another chain, created through a bridge such as LayerZero. If you never send a transaction on Redbelly mainnet, you do not need Redbelly KYC. [7]

Part B

The five common questions

1. When do I need KYC, and when don’t I?

You need KYC to send transactions on Redbelly Network. You do not need it to hold RBNT on an exchange or wrapped RBNT on another chain.

Before a wallet can write to the network, its owner claims an access credential from an accredited issuer and then self-enables the wallet. [1] Averer is the issuer that verifies your identity on Redbelly Access. [5] See the Part A table for each situation.

2. How many wallets can one verified identity cover?

About 10 wallets per verified identity. DAO BRIEF

After you verify and enable your first wallet, Redbelly Access offers to add more wallets connected to the same identity. [5] You do not verify again for each one. What happens beyond the limit is unconfirmed: no official page I could read says. If you reach it, follow Part D and ask Averer. The 10-wallet figure comes from the DAO’s brief, and the Averer page that states it should be linked here before wider sharing. [10]

3. How long does approval usually take?

Unconfirmed. No official page gives a time.

Neither Redbelly’s docs nor Averer’s site state a typical approval time. Do not rely on a number from chat. If you are waiting and worried, see Part D.

4. Can I join from my country?

The DAO: yes, from any region. The network: not if you are in a clause 15.1 jurisdiction.

DAO. The DAO used to restrict regions. That restriction has been removed, and anyone can now join the DAO from any region. DAO BRIEF [10]

Redbelly Network. The network does not offer its services in the jurisdictions below. People who are tax residents of, or located in, those places are not allowed to use the website, platform and tools. OFFICIAL [6]

Afghanistan, Central African Republic, Democratic People’s Republic of Korea, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Guinea-Bissau*, Iran, Iraq, Lebanon, Libya, Myanmar, Russia, Somalia, South Sudan, Sudan, Syria, Ukraine, Yemen, Zimbabwe

*The terms spell this “Guinea-Bussau”. Check the live terms for the current list before you rely on this one.

5. Do I need KYC to stake, and why?

Yes, to stake on Redbelly mainnet. Staking is a network transaction, and only enabled wallets can send them.

Official staking works by calling a staking contract on Redbelly. [8] Every write to the network requires an enabled account. [1] If you stake through an exchange, Redbelly KYC is not part of it and the exchange’s own rules apply. If a third-party staking app says otherwise, treat it as unconfirmed and check with that app.

Part C

Get verified and activate a wallet

Before you start

  • A supported wallet app. Redbelly Access names MetaMask and Coinbase Wallet. [5]
  • A valid passport. Redbelly requires a valid photo ID, verified by biometric checks. [1]
  • A device that can run the identity check. Averer carries out the verification. [5]
  • A little RBNT in the wallet for the enabling transaction. COMMUNITY REPORTED [11] Unconfirmed officially. Redbelly Access says the issuer covers the fees to grant access. [5]
  1. Open Redbelly Access. Go to access.redbelly.network. Check the address carefully. It is the only official place to start. [1]
  2. Connect your wallet. Choose the wallet you want to activate. Use the address you plan to use on Redbelly mainnet. [5]
  3. Verify your identity with Averer. Follow the prompts and prove you hold a valid photo ID, checked with biometrics. Enter your details only on the Redbelly Access flow. [1] [5]
  4. Claim your access credential. Once Averer verifies you, your wallet receives the Redbelly Network Access Credential. [5]
  5. Enable your wallet. Self-enable write access and approve the transaction in your wallet app. The terms refer to this button as “Enable Access”. [1] [6]
  6. Check it worked. The portal should show your account as enabled. Then try a small transaction on Redbelly mainnet.
  7. Add another wallet (optional). After your first wallet is enabled, Redbelly Access offers to add more accounts connected to your identity. Connect the new wallet, follow the prompts, and you do not need to verify again. The limit is about 10 wallets. [5] [10]

Screenshots. This guide has none. The flow requires a real ID check, and a screenshot of it would risk showing personal information. Wording on the live portal can change. If a button name differs from the above, follow the live screen.

Mainnet and testnet are separate. Access is enabled per network, so enable on the one you use. COMMUNITY REPORTED [11]

Part D

When something goes wrong

Approval is taking too long

  1. Make sure you used the official site, access.redbelly.network, and the wallet you plan to use.
  2. Return to the portal with the same wallet to see its status.
  3. No official waiting time exists, so do not guess. If nothing changes, open a ticket with Averer.

My verification was rejected

Redbelly Access tells you to contact Averer for problems with identity verification. [5] Averer also points people to its support portal for help verifying their identity. [9] Open a ticket and say what the portal told you. Redbelly does not publish reasons for rejection, so do not assume one.

I reached the wallet limit

The limit is about 10 wallets per verified identity. DAO BRIEF [10] What happens past it is unconfirmed. Pick the 10 you need most, and ask Averer if you need more.

How to open a support ticket with Averer

  1. Go to averer.co and choose Contact support (top menu) or Go to Support (the “Need help?” section). [9]
  2. Describe the problem in plain words: what you tried, what the portal said, and which wallet address (the public one) is affected.
  3. Never send photos of your ID, seed phrase or private keys in a ticket.

Averer’s own pages say support exists, but I could not read the portal’s direct ticket-form address. Start from averer.co, and replace this step with the direct link once it is confirmed.
